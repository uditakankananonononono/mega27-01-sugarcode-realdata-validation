\paragraph{(F36) Average molecular weight.} $M=\sum_a n_a m_a-(L-1)\,m_{\mathrm{H_2O}}$, with $m_a$ the average residue mass (free amino acid) and one water lost per peptide bond.
\paragraph{(F37) Net charge.} $Z(\mathrm{pH})=\sum_{i\in +}\frac{n_i}{1+10^{\mathrm{pH}-pK_i}}-\sum_{j\in -}\frac{n_j}{1+10^{pK_j-\mathrm{pH}}}$ (Henderson--Hasselbalch per ionisable group, termini included).
\paragraph{(F38) Isoelectric point.} $\mathrm{pI}$ is the root of $Z(\mathrm{pH})=0$; $Z$ is strictly decreasing in pH, so bisection on $[0,14]$ converges, and the residual $6\times10^{-5}$ difference is the bisection tolerance.
\paragraph{(F39) Instability index.} $II=\frac{10}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}\mathrm{DIWV}(x_i,x_{i+1})$ (Guruprasad 1990), and GRAVY $=\frac1L\sum_i h_{KD}(x_i)$ (Kyte--Doolittle).
\paragraph{(F40) Extinction coefficient at 280 nm.} $\varepsilon=5500\,n_W+1490\,n_Y+125\,n_{\mathrm{cystine}}$ M$^{-1}$cm$^{-1}$, with $n_{\mathrm{cystine}}=\lfloor n_C/2\rfloor$ in the oxidised form and $0$ when reduced.
\subsection{Restriction sites: a bug in degenerate recognition sequences}
We digested pBR322 (J01749.1, circular, 4,361 bp) and phage lambda (NC\_001416.1, linear, 48,502 bp) with all 610 enzymes and compared cut positions with Biopython Restriction. Before the fix (sugarcode-ai 699ffee), 533 and 529 enzymes agreed. Two causes explained the disagreements. First, the reverse-strand pattern for a non-palindromic-looking site was built with a plain DNA reverse complement that leaves IUPAC codes unchanged. AccI (GT\^{}MKAC) is in fact palindromic under IUPAC complementation (M$\leftrightarrow$K), but the plain reverse complement GTKMAC was searched as a second pattern, which matches GTGAAC and GTTCAC, neither an AccI site. On pBR322 this gave 5 AccI sites where Biopython finds 2. Second, on linear DNA the finder reported Type IIS cuts that fall outside the molecule. After the fix (sugarcode-ai fe2185d), 608 and 608 enzymes agree. The remaining two, LpnPI, MspJI, are modification-dependent enzymes that do not cut unmethylated DNA; we leave them open.
\paragraph{(F41) IUPAC complement.} The map $c$: A$\leftrightarrow$T, C$\leftrightarrow$G, R$\leftrightarrow$Y, K$\leftrightarrow$M, B$\leftrightarrow$V, D$\leftrightarrow$H, with S, W, N fixed, is the unique involution on the 15 codes that complements every base in the set a code denotes. A site $s$ is palindromic iff $s=\overline{c(s)}$ (reversed), which for AccI holds only under $c$, not under the four-letter map.

\subsection{SAM records and a second bug}

\paragraph{(F45) Phred score.} $Q=-10\log_{10}P_{err}$, encoded as ASCII $Q+33$; the mean read quality reported is the arithmetic mean of $Q$, not $-10\log_{10}$ of the mean error probability, which would be lower for reads with a few bad bases.
\subsection{PDB and mmCIF structures: a third bug}
We parsed 30 RCSB entries in both PDB and mmCIF format (94,121 atom sites, all alternate locations and models) and compared every atom with gemmi 0.7.5. The PDB-format parser was identical in 30/30 entries. The mmCIF parser failed on all 30 real files (error: loop\_ row count 3 is not a multiple of 4 columns (all 30 files)). Two causes: POSIX shell-style splitting broke atom names containing a prime (O5$'$, C1$'$), and loops were closed early when a row contained an unquoted item name as a value, which RCSB files do in the revision-history tables. A CIF 1.1 tokenizer (a quote closes only when followed by whitespace) and closing loops only at row boundaries fixed both (sugarcode-ai fc1fd1e); mmCIF is now identical to gemmi in 30/30 entries. Unit tests with toy CIF strings had passed throughout. Only real files exposed the bug.
\paragraph{(F46) Loop row invariant.} A loop with $c$ columns and $t$ value tokens is well formed iff $t\equiv0\pmod c$; closing the loop at the first reserved-looking token is only safe when the running count $t\bmod c=0$, which is the rule we now apply.

\subsection{BED intervals: a fourth bug}
We fetched two hg38 tracks from the UCSC REST API: CpG islands on chr20 (847 intervals) and RepeatMasker on chr20:0--5\,Mb (11,699 intervals), wrote them as BED and compared bio.bed with bioframe 0.8.0 (merge, overlap). Parsing and 600 random overlap queries agreed exactly (300/300 and 300/300). merge\_intervals did not: its docstring promised bedtools-style merging, yet it refused to join book-ended intervals. RepeatMasker has 3,112 book-ended pairs, so sugarcode returned 11,364 merged intervals where bedtools/bioframe return 8,251. CpG islands have no book-ended pairs and hid the bug (847 = 847). We added a bedtools-compatible min\_dist argument (default 0; $-1$ for overlap-only) and a matching CLI flag; after the fix both tracks match bioframe exactly (8,251 = 8,251).
\paragraph{(F51) Merge rule.} For intervals sorted by start with running end $e^*$, interval $[s,e)$ joins the current block iff $s\le e^*+d$; $d=0$ joins book-ended intervals ($s=e^*$) and $d=-1$ requires at least one shared base ($s<e^*$).
\subsection{GenBank flat files: a fifth bug}
We fetched 25 GenBank records one by one from NCBI nuccore (phage, viral, mitochondrial, bacterial, RefSeqGene and 13 human RefSeq mRNAs) and compared bio.genbank with Biopython 1.88 SeqIO genbank on 1,867 features. Sequences and feature counts already agreed, but only 1,829/1,867 feature locations and 5/302 CDS /translation qualifiers matched. The parser appended every continuation line of a multi-line qualifier to the feature location instead of to the qualifier: /translation kept its first line only, and when a continuation began with digits the location itself was corrupted (E.~coli thrA CDS: 337..2799 became 337..27991). Partial locations (<1..>200) and single bases produced no spans at all. We rewrote qualifier continuation (INSDC joining: no spaces for /translation, one space otherwise, doubled quotes unescaped), added a location parser for join/order, complement, partial ends, between-base sites and remote parts, and kept all IUPAC letters in ORIGIN so coordinates cannot shift. After the fix: spans 1,867/1,867, strands 1,867/1,867, /translation 302/302, CDS extraction 302/302.
The fix exposed a downstream case: KCNQ1's record NG\_016178.2 carries only a partial CDS, complement(<42918..>43038). The old parser read no spans and bio.splice reported 'no CDS spans'; with spans read, the junction map came back 'ok' but empty. We made it fail loud with status 'partial CDS in record'. The splice-region discovery pipeline reads hg38 directly and does not use this parser, so its results are unchanged.
\paragraph{(F52) Qualifier continuation.} For a qualifier whose value spans lines $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_m$, $v=\ell_1\oplus_\sigma\ell_2\oplus_\sigma\cdots\oplus_\sigma\ell_m$ with separator $\sigma=\varnothing$ for /translation and $\sigma=$ one space otherwise; a line starting with / opens a new qualifier only when the running count of quote characters in $v$ is even.

\paragraph{(F53) Frame preservation.} Cleaning must be a letter-wise map $c:\Sigma\to\{A,C,G,T,N\}\cup\{\epsilon\}$ with $c(x)=\epsilon$ only for non-letters; then codon $k$ of the cleaned sequence is $c(s_{3k})c(s_{3k+1})c(s_{3k+2})$ and every downstream codon keeps its frame.
\subsection{k-mer sets and sketches: exact agreement, a biased estimator}
We fetched six coronavirus genomes (SARS-CoV-2, bat RaTG13, pangolin GX-P4L, SARS-CoV Tor2, bat ZC45, MERS) and compared bio.kmer with sourmash 4.8.14 at $k=21$. With sourmash keeping every hash (scaled=1), distinct canonical k-mer counts agreed for 6/6 genomes, and Jaccard and containment agreed exactly on all 15 pairs (maximum difference 0.0).
The sketch estimator is a negative result. bio.kmer's minimizer sketch (window $w=100$, about 600 k-mers per genome) under-estimated Jaccard: for SARS-CoV-2 vs RaTG13 the exact value is 0.321, the minimizer estimate 0.236 and sourmash FracMinHash (scaled=100, about 300 hashes) 0.312. Across the 15 pairs the mean absolute error was 0.0121 for minimizers and 0.0044 for FracMinHash, with half the sketch size. Jaccard over minimizer sets is known to be biased because sequence changes shift which k-mer wins each window; FracMinHash keeps a k-mer if and only if its hash falls below a fixed threshold, so the kept subset is a uniform sample of the union. The module's docstring already labels the values as estimates; we record the bias and do not recommend minimizer Jaccard for distance estimation.
\paragraph{(F54) FracMinHash estimator.} With hash $h$ and threshold $H/s$, $\mathrm{FMH}_s(A)=\{a\in A:h(a)<H/s\}$ and $\hat J=|\mathrm{FMH}_s(A)\cap\mathrm{FMH}_s(B)|/|\mathrm{FMH}_s(A\cup B)|$, which is (nearly) unbiased because inclusion depends on the k-mer alone; the Mash distance is $D=-\tfrac1k\ln\tfrac{2J}{1+J}$.

\subsection{UniProt lookup: a wrong protein and a cache that never hit}
bio.uniprot.search maps a gene symbol to its UniProt entry, and several modules (binding sites, mutation docking) build on it. We checked it against HGNC's curated symbol-to-UniProt mapping for 60 human genes, fetching each HGNC record separately. Before the fix 59/60 matched. The miss was CDKN2A: the query gene:CDKN2A also matches CDKN2A-AS1, and taking the top hit returned Q9UH64 (the antisense-locus protein) instead of P42771 (p16-INK4a). We switched to gene\_exact and prefer the entry whose primary gene name equals the symbol; after the fix 60/60 match and all 60 returned sequences have the length of the entry fetched by accession.
Reading the connector turned up a second bug. The on-disk cache named each file by abs(hash(url)); Python salts string hashes per process, so a new process never found its own cache and offline=True always failed. The same pattern was in the ChEMBL, gnomAD and structure connectors. All four now use SHA-256, and a new process running offline reads TP53 and CDKN2A from cache (P04637, P42771).
\paragraph{(F56) Stable cache key.} A cache key must be a pure function of the request, $k(u)=\mathrm{SHA256}(u)$; a per-process salted hash $k_s(u)$ gives $\Pr[k_{s_1}(u)=k_{s_2}(u)]\approx2^{-64}$ for independent salts, so the hit rate across processes is effectively zero.
\subsection{NCBI Gene lookup: Huntington resolved to the serotonin transporter}
bio.entrez.gene\_id resolves a symbol to an NCBI Gene ID and feeds the gene-analysis module. Against HGNC's curated Gene IDs for the same 60 symbols it scored 58/60. HTT returned 6532, which is SLC6A4 (the serotonin transporter, alias HTT), not huntingtin (3064); TTN returned 7276, which is TTR (transthyretin, alias TTN), not titin (7273). The query field [sym] also matches aliases and the search took the top hit. The function now fetches up to ten hits and returns the one whose official symbol equals the query; after the fix 60/60 match (sugarcode 93d5b12). For a user asking about Huntington disease or titin cardiomyopathy the old answer would have been about the wrong gene.
\paragraph{(F57) Symbol resolution rule.} Given candidate IDs $u_1,\dots,u_r$ from an alias-aware search, return $u^*=u_{\min\{i:\,\mathrm{sym}(u_i)=q\}}$ if that set is non-empty, else $u_1$; an alias match is used only when no official-symbol match exists.

\subsection{gnomAD constraint: a version-2 cutoff on version-4 data}
bio.gnomad.gene\_constraint reads pLI and LOEUF from gnomAD r4 and flags a gene as strongly loss-of-function constrained when LOEUF $<0.35$. That cutoff comes from gnomAD v2. gnomAD's own help page says v4 o/e and LOEUF values run higher than v2, that v2 thresholds do not transfer, and suggests LOEUF $<0.45$ (the most constrained 15\% of 17,063 MANE Select transcripts) for Mendelian interpretation. Under the old cutoff TP53 (LOEUF 0.418, pLI $>0.999$) was called `not strongly constrained'. On our 60 genes the old rule flagged 13 and the v4 rule flags 19; the newly flagged genes are APC, FMR1, LMNA, NF1, SCN5A, TP53. Agreement with the independent pLI $>0.9$ call rose from 51/60 to 57/60 (sugarcode 0a265e7). The sweep also hit gnomAD's API rate limit (HTTP 400, `try again in a few minutes'); the connector used to retry three times within about 1.5\,s, which only burns quota, and now fails fast with a distinct rate-limit error so callers can wait or fall back to the cache.
\paragraph{(F59) LOEUF.} With observed pLoF count $O$ and neutral expectation $E$, $\mathrm{LOEUF}=q_{0.95}(\lambda\mid O)/E$ where the posterior of the true count is $\lambda\mid O\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(O+1,1)$ under a flat prior and Poisson likelihood; a gene is flagged when $\mathrm{LOEUF}<\tau_{v}$ with version-specific $\tau_{v4}=0.45$.
\subsection{Splice-junction maps against ClinVar canonical-site variants}
bio.splice.junction\_map builds donor and acceptor windows, keyed by cDNA position, from each gene's RefSeqGene record, taking the CDS with the most exons as canonical. ClinVar names canonical splice-site variants on the MANE Select transcript (c.N+1, c.N+2, c.N-1, c.N-2), which gives an independent test of both the numbering and the sequence: the named position must be a junction in the map, and the base at the named intronic offset must equal ClinVar's reference allele. We queried 12 genes; CDH1 is not in the module's vendored RefSeqGene list and gets an explicit `no RefSeqGene junction map' status rather than a guess (a coverage limit, noted for follow-up). Across the other 11 (TP53, BRCA1, BRCA2, MLH1, MSH2, APC, PTEN, ATM, PALB2, CFTR, NF1) ClinVar returned 161 such SNVs. The map had the junction for 159 and the right reference base for 159; the two misses are named on other transcripts (KCNK12, a neighbouring gene returned by the MSH2 search, and a non-MANE APC isoform). The `most exons' rule picked the MANE transcript for every gene here, NF1 included. A first run of our own checker scored 96 because it assumed 15 intronic bases in the acceptor window; the windows hold 14 intronic bases plus the first exonic base, consistently across all three map builders. No module bug.
\paragraph{(F60) Window offsets.} For a donor at exon end $b$ the window is $x_{b-2}\dots x_{b+6}$, so intronic position $+k$ is index $2+k$; for an acceptor at exon start $c$ the window is $x_{c-14}\dots x_{c}$, so intronic position $-k$ is index $14-k$ (0-based); on the minus strand both windows are reverse-complemented before indexing.
\subsection{CFD off-target scores}
modules.cfd\_offtarget scores guide/off-target pairs with the Cutting Frequency Determination model. We re-implemented the scoring loop of the authors' published calculator on their released matrices (mismatch\_score.pkl, pam\_scores.pkl) and scored the 600 BRCA1 guide/off-target pairs against all 16 PAM dinucleotides: 9,600/9,600 scores agree, maximum difference 2.2e-16. No bug.
\paragraph{(F61) CFD score.} $\mathrm{CFD}(g,o,p)=\pi(p_2p_3)\prod_{i:\,g_i\ne o_i} m\big(\mathrm{r}g_i{:}\mathrm{d}\overline{o_i},\,i\big)$, where $\overline{o_i}$ is the complement of the off-target base, $m$ the position-specific mismatch activity and $\pi$ the PAM activity.
\subsection{Short tandem repeats: a flank homopolymer hid compound repeats}
modules.str\_scope.find\_strs finds tandem repeats with 1--6 bp units. We ran it and pytrf 1.5.0 (the finder behind Krait and TRF-style pipelines) with pytrf's default minimum repeat counts (12/7/5/4/4/4 for mono- to hexanucleotides) on all 25 GenBank records in our dataset set, from phiX174 to a 193,689-bp RefSeqGene region. Before the fix sugarcode found 208 of pytrf's 211 repeats and nothing pytrf missed. The three losses had one cause: the scan moved left to right and, after taking a short homopolymer, jumped to the end of that run, past the start of an overlapping longer repeat. In HSV-1 (NC\_001806.2) a seven-base G run swallowed the start of (GGGGTT)$_4$; in NG\_005905.2 T and A runs did the same to (TTTTG)$_4$ and (AAAT)$_4$. The finder now takes every maximal periodic run at every unit length and resolves overlaps longest first, trying the other phases of a run before dropping it (sugarcode 727344a). After the fix it finds 211/211, with 0 extra calls; 209 have identical coordinates and the other 2 are the same span reported in the disease-motif phase (CGG rather than GCG in PTEN's 5' UTR), which the module does on purpose.
The same module labels repeat genotypes. The old rule used only the distance from a normal upper bound (normal if $\le2$ above it, intermediate up to 10 above, else `pathogenic-range candidate'). Compared with the GeneReviews allele tables it mislabels HTT CAG counts 27, 28, 36 (27 and 28 are intermediate, 36 is reduced-penetrance pathogenic) and 154 FMR1 CGG counts between 5 and 300, from 47 to 200: every premutation allele (55--200) was called a pathogenic expansion, which in counselling terms confuses a carrier with an affected full-mutation allele. expansion\_call now takes an optional locus and uses the published bands for HTT and FMR1 (0 disagreements on HTT 5--120); without a locus the old behaviour is unchanged.
\paragraph{(F62) Maximal periodic runs.} For unit length $u$, position $j$ is periodic if $x_j=x_{j+u}$; a maximal run of periodic positions $[a,b)$ defines the span $[a,b+u)$ holding $\lfloor (b+u-a-k)/u\rfloor$ whole units from phase $k\in\{0,\dots,u-1\}$. Candidates over all $u$ are kept greedily by length (ties: smaller $u$, then leftmost) subject to disjoint whole-unit intervals, which is the rule pytrf-style finders apply implicitly by reporting the longest repeat at each locus.
\subsection{Bayesian ACMG scoring against ClinGen expert panels}
modules.acmg\_bayesian scores ACMG/AMP criteria with the Tavtigian et al.\ (2018) Bayesian model in its point form (supporting 1, moderate 2, strong 4, very strong 8; pathogenic $\ge10$, likely pathogenic 6--9, likely benign $-1$ to $-6$, benign $\le-7$). The ClinGen Evidence Repository publishes, for every variant a Variant Curation Expert Panel (VCEP) has classified, the criteria it applied and its final call. We used 7,929 current classifications from 33 panels across 117 genes (the server closed the bulk download after about 18 MB, so only complete rows were used). ClinGen writes modified strengths as suffixes: PM2\_Supporting, PVS1\_Strong, PM3\_Very Strong, BS1\_Stand Alone. The module accepted a strength only as a separate field, so in 5,699 rows at least one code was rejected, and it reproduced 4,583/7,929 panel calls. The rejections were listed in the output rather than hidden, but anyone pasting ClinGen notation got a wrong class. The parser now reads the suffix, and a stand-alone benign strength acts like BA1 (sugarcode cdf160b). The module now reproduces 7,515/7,929 calls (94.8\%), with no rejected codes.
As a baseline we coded the original ACMG/AMP 2015 combining rules (Richards et al., Table 5), counting each criterion at its applied strength and calling conflicting evidence uncertain. They reproduce 7,267/7,929 (91.7\%); the point model is closer to what the panels actually do. The largest remaining disagreement is 229 variants the panels call uncertain and the model calls likely benign; 177 of them net to exactly $-1$ point, typically a lone BP4. The 2015 rules need two benign supporting criteria for likely benign, and the panels follow that; the 2018 posterior bands do not. We report this as a known difference and did not change the bands. BA1 with pathogenic criteria also met is rare and panels treat it differently (21 benign, 3 pathogenic, 3 uncertain significance, 4 likely benign), so the module keeps BA1 as an override but flags the conflict.
\paragraph{(F63) Point form of the Bayesian model.} With prior $\pi=0.1$ and $O_{PVSt}=350$, a criterion of strength $k$ points has odds $350^{k/8}$; summed points $S$ give posterior $P(S)=\dfrac{\tfrac{\pi}{1-\pi}350^{S/8}}{1+\tfrac{\pi}{1-\pi}350^{S/8}}$, so $P(10)=0.994$, $P(6)=0.900$, $P(-1)=0.051$ and $P(-7)=0.0007$.

modules.openclinvar infers a variant's molecular consequence from its HGVS name when the name alone decides it, and abstains otherwise. We pulled 720 ClinVar records (60 per gene for BRCA1, BRCA2, TP53, MLH1, MSH2, CFTR, APC, PTEN, LDLR, MYH7, SCN5A and RYR1) and scored the module's call as correct when ClinVar lists that consequence for the record. \paragraph{(F64) Segment rule for c. ranges.} Write each end as $(a,s,k)$: anchor $a$ (with region 5\textquotesingle{} UTR, CDS or 3\textquotesingle{} UTR), intron side $s\in\{+,-,\varnothing\}$ and offset $k$. \subsection{CRISPR on-target scores against measured guide activity}

\paragraph{(F65) Spearman correlation.} For scores $s$ and measurements $y$ with ranks $R(s_i)$, $R(y_i)$, $\rho=\mathrm{corr}\big(R(s),R(y)\big)$, which for untied data equals $1-\dfrac{6\sum_i d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)}$ with $d_i=R(s_i)-R(y_i)$; the per-gene median guards against between-gene differences in dynamic range.

\subsection{Elastic network modes: a doubled Hessian diagonal}
modules.evofold\_4d.anm\_modes computes anisotropic network model (ANM) modes from C$\alpha$ coordinates; its fluctuation profile is offered as a B-factor proxy and feeds hinge detection. We built the ANM Hessian for the first chain of all 30 PDB structures in our set with ProDy 2.4.1 (cutoff 10\,\AA, $\gamma=1$; ProDy's own eigen-solver fails with the installed SciPy, so both Hessians were diagonalised with numpy). Before the fix no structure matched: the module looped over ordered residue pairs and subtracted each contact block from both diagonal blocks on both visits, so every diagonal block was doubled, rows of the Hessian no longer summed to zero and the six rigid-body zero modes disappeared. The `lowest modes' it reported were therefore not the collective motions of the protein; the fluctuation profile correlated with ProDy's at $r$ as low as 0.19, and with crystallographic B-factors at median 0.28. Each contact is now added once (sugarcode feced35). All 30/30 structures now give ProDy's six lowest non-zero frequencies to the module's four reported decimals, with fluctuation profiles correlated at $r\ge1.0000$; the median B-factor correlation is 0.38, the same as ProDy's (0.38). 2 chains have more than six zero modes because of chain breaks; the module skips all zero modes, which is the right behaviour.
\paragraph{(F69) ANM Hessian.} For contacts $r_{ij}<r_c$ with unit vector $\hat e_{ij}$, $H_{ij}=-\gamma\,\hat e_{ij}\hat e_{ij}^{\top}$ for $i\ne j$ and $H_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}H_{ij}$, so each block row sums to zero and $H$ has six zero eigenvalues (three translations, three rotations) for a connected network; mean-square fluctuations are $\langle\Delta r_i^2\rangle\propto\sum_{k}\lVert u_k^{(i)}\rVert^2/\lambda_k$ over non-zero modes.

\paragraph{(F70) Shrake--Rupley estimate.} Atom $i$ with expanded radius $R_i=r_i+r_p$ carries $N$ near-uniform sphere points $x_{ik}$; its area is $A_i=4\pi R_i^2\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_k\mathbf{1}\big[\lVert x_{ik}-c_j\rVert\ge R_j\ \forall j\ne i\big]$, with sampling error $O(N^{-1/2})$ per atom that largely cancels in residue and molecule totals.
\subsection{Vina-form docking score against AutoDock Vina}
The same module's vina\_score claims the AutoDock Vina 1.1 functional form on a fixed pose. Reading Vina's source showed three departures. To test the pair terms numerically with no atom-typing ambiguity we scored stacked all-carbon rings, where every atom is a Vina hydrophobic carbon, with AutoDock Vina 1.2.7 in score-only mode. Before the fix the module gave -1.5646, -0.8557 and -0.3662 kcal/mol at 3.6, 4.0 and 4.6\,\AA\ separation where Vina gives -2.31, -1.65 and -0.634. The rotor division is checked against the source only. Element-level typing still cannot separate polar from apolar carbon or place donors without hydrogens, so on real complexes the score remains an approximation, as the module says.
\paragraph{(F71) Vina score.} With surface distance $s_{ij}=r_{ij}-R^{XS}_i-R^{XS}_j$, $e=\sum_{ij}\big[w_1e^{-(s/0.5)^2}+w_2e^{-((s-3)/2)^2}+w_3\,s^2\mathbf 1[s<0]+w_4h(s)+w_5b(s)\big]$ over pairs within 8\,\AA, and $\Delta G=e/(1+w_{rot}N_{rot})$ with $w=(-0.0356,-0.0052,0.840,-0.0351,-0.587)$, $w_{rot}=0.0585$; $h$ is 1 for $s<0.5$ falling to 0 at 1.5, $b$ is 1 for $s<-0.7$ falling to 0 at 0.
\subsection{Library-evaluation graph keys against RDKit canonical SMILES}
molecule\_eval deduplicates generated libraries with an order-invariant graph digest (four rounds of Weisfeiler--Lehman relabelling over element, aromaticity and charge, then a sorted edge list) rather than canonical SMILES. A digest like this must give the same key for every SMILES spelling of a molecule and different keys for different molecules. We tested both on 993 ChEMBL phase-4 drugs, comparing against RDKit 2026.03.6 canonical SMILES with stereochemistry removed, and wrote three randomised SMILES per drug. Before the fix 3 drugs changed key under re-spelling (acetazolamide CHEMBL19 among them). All three were written by ChEMBL with a directional bond marker between two aromatic ring atoms, as in \texttt{/N=c1/sc}. The shared SMILES parser read every / or \textbackslash{} as an explicit single bond, which broke the aromatic ring; RDKit keeps such a bond aromatic. After the fix (sugarcode 15a8ba7), where the marker only sets direction and bond order follows the implicit rule, there are 0 invariance failures, 0 keys shared by molecules RDKit calls different, and 0 RDKit molecules split across keys; both give 980 unique structures. The directional-bond change left the QSAR descriptor and Morgan benchmarks unchanged when re-run. Internal diversity (mean pairwise Tanimoto distance, radius-2 2048-bit fingerprints, 300 drugs, all pairs) is 0.9055 with the module's fingerprint and 0.8994 with RDKit's. Four WL rounds can in principle merge graphs that differ only beyond radius four; no such case occurred in this set.
\paragraph{(F72) Internal diversity.} For a library of $n$ unique molecules with fingerprints $f_i$, $D=\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\big(1-T(f_i,f_j)\big)$ with $T(a,b)=|a\wedge b|/(|a|+|b|-|a\wedge b|)$; the module samples at most $10^4$ pairs and bootstraps a 95\% interval.
\subsection{Nussinov folding against an independent implementation, exhaustive oracle and ViennaRNA}
riboswitch uses an exact Nussinov dynamic program (maximum number of nested base pairs, minimum loop 3) to score designed switches. We checked the recursion three ways. On 100 short sequences (length up to 16) we enumerated every non-crossing matching by brute force; the module matched the exhaustive optimum on all 100 (the oracle itself needed two fixes during the check - reused positions and a stale pruning bound - which is why we keep it in the repository). On 145 real Rfam seed sequences from four riboswitch families (TPP RF00059, FMN RF00050, glycine RF00504, cobalamin RF00174), an independently written Nussinov recurrence (pair-$i$-with-$k$ decomposition instead of the module's bifurcation form) agreed on the optimal pair count for all 145 (0 mismatches). \subsection{Prime-editing guide design against published, validated pegRNAs}
prime\_design builds pegRNAs: a 20-nt spacer, a 3' extension of reverse-transcription template (RTT) plus primer-binding site (PBS). Three departures from the published anatomy were found by replaying two experimentally validated pegRNAs through the module. (i) The PBS was reverse-complemented out of the RT-template region, so PBS and RTT overlapped; the PBS must anneal to the 3' flap of the nicked strand, i.e. it is the reverse complement of the protospacer's PAM-proximal end. (ii) For plus-strand PAMs the RTT started at the edit, not at the nick. (iii) When the RT template was truncated to the chosen length, the module kept the nick-distal end; reverse transcriptase reads from the PBS, so the RTT's 3' end must pair with the nick base. After the fixes (sugarcode 947b55e) the module reproduces both published extensions exactly: HEK3 +1 CTT insertion (Anzalone et al. 2019; Addgene 132778; also validated by pegFinder, Chow et al. 2021) RTT \texttt{TCTGCCATCAAAGC} + PBS \texttt{GTGCTCAGTCTG}, and RNF2 +5 G$>$T RTT \texttt{AACGAACACATCAGG} + PBS \texttt{TAATGACTAAGATG}. Before the fix the 'PBS' came out as \texttt{TCTGCCATCAAA} - RTT sequence, not flap. End to end, from the HEK3 genomic context (Anzalone substrate AVA024) with the +1 CTT edit, the top design uses the published spacer and a PBS from the correct flap (10 nt auto-chosen by the Wallace-$T_m$ rule vs the published 12 nt; same annealing tract), and its RTT contains the published template at the nick. A crash on insertion edits with empty ref was fixed along the way. The PBS/RTT boundary convention (spacer positions 17/18, one nt PAM-proximal of the canonical Cas9 nick) was adopted to match every published validated pegRNA we could locate, including Anzalone's own in-vitro pegRNAs.
